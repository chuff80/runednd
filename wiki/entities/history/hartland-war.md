# The Hartland War

`Canon status:` This page is a curated synthesis from runesite canon pages and narrative drafts; exact year-by-year sequencing varies across sources.

## Overview

The Hartland War is treated across sources as the defining civilization-level conflict of the setting: a genocidal struggle initiated under Cyric's leadership that shattered earlier human-Sidhe-Tuatha coexistence. The war culminated in Cyric's defeat by Darian Bloodstone at Dragonshome Peak, but its aftermath produced the Gaeas, mass memory loss, demographic collapse, and centuries of political fragmentation.

## Belligerents and Key Figures

- Human war factions aligned to [[entities/characters/01-origin-figures/cyric.md|Cyric the Warlord]].
- Tuatha and Sidhe war coalitions, including major commanders such as Badb and Celestina in later recollection.
- Royal/anti-Cyric human forces tied to [[entities/characters/01-origin-figures/darian.md|Darian Bloodstone]].
- Secondary theaters and successor forces centered in [[entities/locations/atania.md|Atania]], [[entities/locations/ghealdar.md|Ghealdar]], and the Hartland region.

## Timeline and Turning Points

1. **Pre-war fracture**: political and cultural relations between humans and Sidhe/Tuatha degrade under fear narratives and power consolidation around Cyric.
2. **Escalation to genocide**: sources describe deliberate anti-Tuatha/Sidhe campaigns, mass casualties, and Ogham-enabled devastation on multiple fronts.
3. **Ghealdar catastrophe**: major urban destruction and undead-related atrocities become emblematic of war brutality.
4. **Dragonshome climax**: Darian Bloodstone defeats Cyric at Dragonshome Peak; traditions also link this moment to phylactery-related uncertainty.
5. **Postwar containment**: the Gaeas and related settlements end open war but impose long-term memory and identity rupture across mortal populations.

## Outcomes and Lasting Impact

- Severe demographic and institutional collapse across races and regions.
- Long-term weakening and isolation of Tuatha/Sidhe communities in many accounts.
- Transformation of Atania and surrounding territories into post-imperial successor polities.
- Persistent historical dispute over Darian's role, Cyric's survival risk, and responsibility for postwar settlements.
- Continued influence on modern-era politics, resistance rhetoric, and Ogham governance.
