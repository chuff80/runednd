The orcish martial empire of Gibbard was the only nation intact when the arch-djinn were freed upon the death of the The Raven Queen. After the elemental-wrought calamities died down, Gibbard had an occupying force in Ghealdar. Their rulers were a series of shogun-style warlords that eventually fell into infighting, with a warlord claiming control of the fog-shrouded djinn woods and the other orcs dividing up into factions.

The orcs brought order to the Free States of Doon, Adare, and Toomevara. Those cities submitted to suzerainty and they persisted as allies to Gibbard after the empire collapsed, albeit at a diplomatic distance.

The presence of Al-Abyad, the Water Arch-Djinn, also altered the nature of the Hartland Forest, and the orcs' biology was influenced by it. Many orcs were born with strange birth defects that allowed them to breathe water or dissolve into mist and reappear in another location. These changes created cracks in the hierarchy of orcish society.

This led to a centuries-long period of civil war. Eventually a warlord, Supreme Commander Kayleigh the 2nd , unified the people around 500 AD (After the Djinn) and a policy of isolation was implemented, with non-orcs being banished from the woods.

As the orcs became more and more successful, their rigid command structure began to collapse under its own weight, lacking the direction of a nation at war, and the orcs again fell to petty infighting and Kayleigh's successors were unable to hold the Empire together. The collapse was slow, and then sudden as Atania and Ghealdar saw opportunities to make their own fortune. When both city-states issued proclamations that they would no longer pay tribute to the Empire, the orcs were unable to enforce their will and the nation officially relinquished control and withdrew into their own borders.

Origins

The Orcish nation of Gibard was created when Komang the Warlord united the Orcish tribes into organized war bands and overran the elven nation in the Hartland nearly 800 years ago. In the aftermath, Komang turned his mighty host into a nation. The orcs, after spending centuries as roving marauders, surprisingly ordered themselves into a hierarchical society. That nation cleared large swaths of the forest to farm and build fortified villages.

Komang, in addition to being a large and mighty warrior, was a visionary organizer. The hierarchy he created turned into the structure his descendants would use to keep the short-lived, warlike people from turning on themselves. Society is organized around mandatory military service when an orc reaches their majority at age ten.

All orcs enter the military at the rank of Orcling, and rise through the ranks based on meritorious service, honor in battle, and organizational skill. While ancient orcs thrived on the chaos and blood of battle, the structure of their society rewarded harnessing and channeling that energy. Now they maintain the form of that structure, but much of the nation is trapped in petty bureauracy.

Orcish Military Organization

The Gibard military is organized in units of ten. Ten solders make a unit. Ten units makes a squad. Ten squads make a battalion. Ten battalions make a legion.

Orcish society is rigid and social interactions are governed by an orc's rank. Lower ranked orcs always defer to higher ranked orcs in decisions, down to who speaks first.

The only beings who exist outside of this rigid hierarchy are the Millteach , orcish war wizards, and the Sagart , the shamans. The Millteach have officers in their ranks, but they only answer to their own command structure unless they are assigned to a specific unit. In that instance, they obey the unit's commanding officer. The senior Millteach reports to the Commander of Gibard.

Sagart, the Orcish Spirit Channelers and Shamans, exist completely outside of the military. When an orcling is picked by a shaman to undergo initiation, they are placed outside of orcish society, sometimes a traumatic experience for the orcling. The Sagart is the only part of Gibard that is seemingly non-hierarchical. An unspoken aspect of orcish culture is that many orcling initiates are born gender non-conforming. Their nonbinary nature marks them as set aside by the Spirits.

The orcs welcomed the return of the arch-djinn, who they call The Great Spirits. What other cultures called the Elemental Apocalypse is known among Gibard shamans as The Great Charge . The shamans see the event as an auspicious time, despite the many orcs who died and the rise of a terrifying mist elemental reanimating the corpses of the green dragons throughout the Hartland forest.

Murderball

Orcs are raised communally. Regardless of who their parents are, any orcish mother can discipline a wayward child. Meals are served communally from the time of breastfeeding. Most orcs see each other as cousins, and the orclings they enter the military with are usually considered as close as brother and sister.

But brother and sister can frequently drive each other nuts. That, combined with the natural orcish aggression, used to mean that many orcish children died before adulthood. An enterprising orcish Commander invented a game to channel the energy of orclings. Murderball looks like chaos, but is closer to a highly structured game of King of the Hill where the hill is a pile of heaving, shoving orcs.

Important Orcs & Landmarks

Commander Kayleigh. The leader of the orcs at the beginning of the Great Destruction, Kayleigh led the orcs into what is increasingly seen as their golden era and is seen as the early visionary of the great orc empire. The orcs who deny the collapse of the nation invoke her name as a rallying cry to bring back Gibbard's dominance.

Silvermane. One of the Dragon Talkers and friend to Briar, the Saint of the Church of the Creator. Silvermane is seen as the greatest hero of the orcish nation after Komang the Founder. Silvermane was rumored to be among the first orcs to be born with the blessing of Al-Abyad.
