Dragonshome Peak and Dragonshome Circle - the peak of Dragonshome mountain is where Darian Bloodstone destroyed Cyric the Warlord in the Hartland War. The original legends suggest that Cyric's phylactery was flung from the mountain into the inaccessible Circle upon Cyric's death. Darian's body was laid to rest inside the mountain, and is guarded by the spirit of his dragon mount, Pyraxus. Dragonshome Circle itself is a vast valley rumored to be the home of the leading council of dragons. Dragon Council Leadership includes: Pyraxen – descendant of Pyraxis, adult golden dragon, Silvaran, a Silver, Fen, a Bronze, Quith, a Copper, Fayethl, a Red, Bardentl, a Blue and Shneztl, a Green.

Kjeldor - the mountain kingdom of the fair-haired Kjeldoran elves. These tribal, griffin-riding elves have resisted the technological changes so common to the human lands. They use magic to help their griffins, and to secure their homes from earthquakes and landslides. The Elves maintain their ancestral ways, including yearly vigils for the ousted Hartland elves.

Halfling Hills – For centuries, the halflings were wild cannibals who lived on the knife’s edge of danger between the barren hills and the horrific things that emerge from the Saeserin swamp, their society has evolved and only a tiny number of halflings still practice cannibalism. Jaia, the halfling Paladin convert to the Church of the Great Creator, has a sarcophagus that is guarded at all times by her disciples and the Church has a small temple there. Since the invasion by the lizard folk, and the help of the Church in repelling that invasion, the halflings have fully embraced the Creator and have become some of the church's most ardent missionaries.

In the modern world, the halflings have embraced technological advancements, going through a period of building small hillside villages that were lush and easy to garden. The halflings became quiet and happy...and fat. Eating became the thing most halflings were obsessed with and halfling cuisine became world-famous. Many Gibbard rulers and wealthy merchants hired halfling chefs.

As the nation continued getting fatter, some of the jungle halflings saw a need for a reformation and began preaching the need for fitness and preparation. They encouraged halflings to get in shape, to work and guard the world against the horrors of the Black City in the Saesserin swamp. They pointed to the halfling boar-riding paladin Jaia as an example of a hero.

Those teachings became twisted over time. While some halflings became warriors, their culture twisted the teachings into a 'don't get fat' message. Recently, a halfling artificer named Cadiere invented a device that prevents the stomach from expanding beyond a certain size. He introduced it with the blessing of a cleric of the Church of the Creator, and many halflings saw this as an easy way to stay slim, if not strong.

Since then, halfling culture has come to center around the appearance of fitness. Most halfling leaders are slim and well-coifed. Salons have cropped up in many nations that specialize in styling halfling's feet hair. These shops are mostly run by the jungle halflings, who maintain their own traditional religious and fitness practices. They eschew the Order of Briar, for unknown reasons, but otherwise are a strong mix of Church of the Creator adherents and followers of the various elemental religions.

The Saeserin Swamp – Once home to dozens of tribes of lizard folk, the Saeserin is now an organized nation under the Dead City, an obsidian fortress that was built in the heart of the dark jungle. The Dead City is rumored to be an Unseelie Fey outpost or perhaps the source of shadowy beings previously never seen on Rune. Eight centuries ago, an army of lizard folk invaded the Halfling hills, touching off a major war between the Lizard Folk and the Halflings. When the Church intervened, the lizardfolk were repelled and the Dead City negotiated a truce with the Church. Very few non-lizardfolk enter the Saesarin swamp.

Spine of the World - This mountain range extends North from Dragonshome peak, cutting through the center of the world of Rune. Home to multiple tribes of giants, the mountains are one of the last places in the world where the Church of the Creator is not widely worshipped. The giant thanes officially forbid missionaries from the Church, and most of the small folk, with the exception of the halfling missionaries who sneak in illegally, refuse to journey into the mountains.
