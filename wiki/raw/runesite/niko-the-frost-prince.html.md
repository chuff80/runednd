Five hundred years as a fey lord have changed Niko. At first, totally overwhelmed with this responsibility that he felt had been forced on him, he retreated into his new realm. In the first few years he nearly convinced himself that it wasn’t true, that making him a fey lord had been a lie of the Summer Queen, part of some elaborate but failed strategy. But as the land thawed around him and life began to return he could no longer deny that he was having some effect on it.  Then he was angry. He spent years in a rage, stalking across his realm screaming into the sky, tearing ditches into the earth with his bare hands as if he could punish the land itself. Eventually the rage became too much to sustain and when it did Niko gave himself over to despair. He crawled into a hole in the rock of the mountain and wept. He has always been close the spirits of wild places and he had always taken comfort in the thought that one day he would join them. He mourned the loss of his mortality, of the life he once had, of all the familiar things that were lost to him now. He doesn't know how long he spent like that, wandering across the mountains, keeping himself alive in some mechanical, animal way, but dead to the world and to himself.

One day he was sitting by a rocky outcrop, looking at nothing in particular. He had been here before, but this time he found himself looking at the plants. They were wilted and sick, collapsing under the weight of their dying leaves. They looked like they were barely able to pull any sustenance from the soil, just enough to survive but not enough to flourish. In that moment he woke up, he realized that this was his fault. His frustration and despair had seeped into the soil and been taken up by the plants and now they were suffering. He could imagine animals eating those plants and that suffering becoming their own, spreading deeper and further until the land was a barren wasteland again. He also realized that he had the power to change that. He picked himself up and decided he could live for the land around him and he threw all his energy into revitalizing his realm.

Over the next hundred years Niko’s realm began to flourish. Forests covered the slopes of the mountains and animals returned. During this time, Niko was able to reconcile himself with his position. He knew he could not hide in the mountains forever. He could sulk as long as he liked but it would not change the fact that now he was a fey lord, the Frost Prince. He thought about the dealings he had had with the Fey Court, he remembered how resistant to action they had been, how unwilling to change and it occurred to him that he might be able to do something different. As a member of the Fey Court he would have power, his voice would matter in a way that it never had before. He had resources at his disposal, things he could use to act on his own agenda towards his own goals. The more he thought about it, the more the idea appealed to him and so he made up his mind. He would take his place in the Fey Court, be the Frost Prince. It was that or sulk in the woods for a suddenly greatly extended lifetime.

As the newest member of the Court and one whose membership was gained as part of a coup, many others approached Niko with no small amount of distrust and suspicion. He expected as much and knew it would take some time before he would be able to consider any of them allies (with the exception of The Lady) so he focused much of his attention building relationships with minor fey nobles and other well placed individuals outside the nobility. Among the Archfey he has gained a reputation for being stubborn and contrary, although this does not always mean he will vote that way. That he can be so difficult to read, seemingly by intention, has frustrated more than a few of the members of the Court. His reputation is, too some, further damaged by the fact that Tinuviel is the only other of the Archfey beyond the Lady that Niko sees regularly outside the official meetings of the Court. They have met both in the city of Cendriane and at Niko’s Lodge in the mountains, though whether they are actual political allies, or just drinking buddies, remains difficult to determine.

While he has not yet made many moves in the Court itself, there are few who could deny that Niko has been active, though they might have trouble pinpointing, or proving, exactly what he has been up to. Even as a mortal, he prefered to avoid open conflict when he could, not fighting unless that was the only option available to him. Now as a fey lord, this has not changed and he prefers to find quieter, cleaner methods of achieving his goals. He worries that the increasingly common nature of magic and technology in the mortal realm will cause natural systems to be thrown out of harmony and the people will start to view the land in which they live as a resource to be used rather than a larger entity they have a relationship with. He also, however, is worried that too much interference by the Fey in the mortal world could lead to other problems that could potentially destabilize the Fey and mortal kingdoms. In all things Niko tries to take a long view and acts in ways that will have the greatest effect for the smallest impact. It is easier to pull out a sapling growing too close to a house than to try to remove a tree whose roots have already damaged the foundations. Niko makes sure to keep informed about events both in the Feywild and the mortal realm. To that end, people like Theobald are always welcome at Niko’s Lodge, but there are also more than a few functionaries, secretaries, and gossips in fey and mortal courts who pass along information to people who eventually pass it along to the Frost Prince.

In his realm, Niko maintains two residences, the hall of the former Frost Prince and his own Lodge out in the forest; called the Hall and the Lodge respectively. The Hall he uses for formal meetings or a place to speak with people he doesn't particularly like or trust. The Lodge he considers to be is home and prefers to do business there with people he wouldn’t mind seeing even if there wasn’t any business to be done. Aside from the settlements of the giants, there are no other developed areas in Niko’s lands, though his forests are far from empty. The snow tigers who have taken up residence there help to serve as a deterrent to the wanderings of the uninvited.

The elves at the base of the mountains have remained a concern for Niko and he is still unsure what he will do about them, though he knows he cannot put off a final decision for too much longer. So far he is content to keep them in their refugee settlement and out of his forests. He has little sympathy for the elves’ plight and firmly believes the war they are fighting against the orcs a foolish one. The elves believe the Hartland Forest belongs to them, but Niko knows that is nothing but their own arrogant entitlement talking. The elves lost nothing but what they lacked the strength to hold on to and the orcs have as much right to live on that land as anyone else. When the elves lost it and were unable to take it back within a reasonable amount of time, they should have looked for other places in the mortal realm to make a home for themselves. That they continue to fight a war for so long and still be no closer to victory has only sealed their fate and ensured their destruction as a people. He is not convinced that even giving them more land in his realm would sway them, but would instead fuel this cultural madness that seems to have seeped into their collective psyche. He fears they would use whatever land he might allow them as a staging ground for large, more destructive pushes into orc territory and that this could eventually force him to become involved in a conflict he wants no part in. He lies the blame for this squarely at the feet of the commanders and elders among the elves who have continued to push this agenda for centuries to the detriment of their people. So far he has not allowed the elves to build any more settlements beyond the ones they already have, but he has no desire to watch them die on his doorstep. He allows them to enter his forests to hunt and gather wood as they need it, knowing that the tigers’ aggressive, territorial natures will keep elven visits quick and sporadic.
