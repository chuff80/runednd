The Church of the Great Creator is still the most visible and dominant of the churches, but after the Great Destruction, many additional faiths have come into existence.

Elementalism

After the Great Destruction, when massive storms and earthquakes shook the entire continent, many began paying homage to the Djinn believed to be most linked to their respective elements. Barqan (air), Manmun (earth), Al-abyad (water), Al-ahmar (fire) are the most common djinni worshipped, but there are many others.

The sects' centers of worship are the upper Hartland (Barqan), Atania (Manmun), Al-Abyad (Ghealdar), and Kjeldor (Al-ahmar). There are shrines to each djinn in each place, and each shrine attracts religious pilgrimages from all over the world, with the exception of elves being forbidden from entering the Hartland forest, including the elvish nation of Kjeldor.

Barqan's worship centers in orcish traditions, with a deep mix of forestry motifs and elvish symbolism left over after Gibbard drove the elves from the forest.

Al-Abyad's holiest shrine is centered atop an island in the midst of the Great River delta at Ghealdar. Once the 2nd largest city on the continent, the almost total destruction of Atania left Ghealdar as the easiest place to ship goods into the mainland continent. Al-Abyad's worship is fashionable in Ghealdar, with most leaders at least paying lip service to the djinn of water. Priests wear a wide variety of vestments and symbols from the world over, with a recurring motif of tridents and waves, symbolizing the destruction and peace of water.

Manmun's order is based in Atania, with many small shrines and a large altar in the Northeastern corner of the Old City. The order's priesthood is much more formal than the other orders, reflecting the influence of the Church of the Creator. Many of the order's early priests were former clerics of the Creator, including the early goblin engineers turned priests. The religious motifs include tumbling stones.

Al-ahmar's order is based in Kjeldor, with its most important shrine atop a mountain near the border between Kjeldor and Gibbard. Orcish pilgrims are discouraged but not expressly forbidden from entering that area of Kjeldor. Most orcs will not enter the area due to the danger to their lives from elvish reactionaries who still believe the elves to be at war with Gibbard.

Church of the Great Creator

After the Great Destruction, many people came to believe that the Creator was no longer with the world. They saw Him as a distant deity who cared nothing for the lives of his worshippers, or as a malevolent being who sent the storms and earthquakes as a form of sadistic experimentation.

Some of the church's priests left to worship the elemental spirits. Some gave up their faith completely. Some joined sects that were devoted to reform or even directly attacking the church, seeking to undermine its power and bring it down.

Despite the rise of the sects, the church persists and continues being the largest religion in Dhomaine an Duine.

The current head of the church is Aemon Ban the 4th .

The Order of Briar . In rural or small towns, the ancient Order of Briar, formerly the Order of the Grave, is remembered for caretaking graveyards. In the major cities like Ghealdar and Atania, Briar is revered for her role in sacrificing herself to escort The Raven Queen into the afterlife, saving the world from complete destruction, but also unleashing the Archdjin, causing the Great Destruction.

The Holy Order of Trajean. The inquisitors of the church, the Trajeanites are in charge of maintaining doctrinal purity within the priesthood.

The Order of St Grok. An educational order led by goblin engineers, many of whom are skilled with arcane and divine magic.

The Council on the Fey. Deals with thorny issues around the power of the Archfey vs. the Creator, reconciling racial traditions with church doctrine, and ambassadorships to the various nations.

Religion In Ages Past (Before-Destruction)

The oldest religions, the worship of the Spirits of the Elements, have faded since the Reconciliation, but still maintain a strong presence throughout Atania and the Free States. They are especially established in the Dwarven Kingdom and the Kingdom of Gibard. Their priests manifest their powers in keeping with the beings that they worship. The most commonly worshiped spirits are: Gaoth, a spirit of wind; Tintreach a being of energy and electricity; Mearog, a being of stone and earth who only appears underground; Griosagh, a flame spirit blamed for natural disasters and revered by blacksmiths and Aigean, the Ocean.

There are also dozens of lesser known spirits that are revered, feared, or called upon to curse and bless people.  Each of these spirits make up important parts of the day to day lives of the people. They are invoked as curses, wishes, and daily charms.

Since the Reconciliation, the ascendent religion of the Church of the Great Creator has taken over as the primary religion of the people, and has the most powerful voice in the government of Atania. The people of Atania remember the church's role in helping bring down the Emperor Gabalifix, using their knights and priests to physically rescue and protect the women and children who were about to be slaughtered at Gabalifix's Horror in the Square of Atania.

600 years ago, the church built an enormous temple in the heart of Atania. It’s a massive edifice – fortress-like, and it holds not only the clergy, but also the paladins and the monks that were trained there. It's the seat of power of the church, and the second most important structure in Atania, behind the palace itself.

The founder of the church, Aemon Ban , was a monk who lived a hard life of self-denial. Aemon claimed to have traveled beyond the Great Divide to the Gaineamhlach, a great desert, and learned about the Great Creator there. Initially the Church was extremely benevolent, helping the people of Atania become more prosperous. As Aemon aged, however, other church leaders began taking a more day-to-day role and are enforcing a more strict gospel of austerity and self-denial. The church's decision to save the people of Atania was the cause of the great schism in the church between the leaders who preach austerity and self-denial, and the leaders who preach ministry to the sick and kindness to the poor.
