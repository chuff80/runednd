As the sun peaked over the mountains that Surrounded Atania their rays began to filter through the homes of the families that lived there. In one home, the sun tickled the eyelids of one Brok Kastis. As the man sat up, the straw in his bed crunched and snapped under his shifting weight, he could smell the dew outside the window on the ground and he could see the rays of light that illuminated the particles of dust that hung swirling in the air. While he sat there with his feet hanging over the side of the bed but not quite reaching the floor, he considered what he should make for breakfast; it only took a moment, he decided on mush. That was all he really wanted. It was too early for a full meal and further he did not have the luxury to spend all day in the kitchen.

He quickly set to building a fire in the stove and to preparing his meal. While he waited for the cold stove to heat his mind wondered to the happenings of the city that had occurred over the past 20 years. He had just been a child that night. Although he was merely 4 years old, he could still recall with vivid detail the night that the monsters had attacked his city. He had been chasing a grey wolf like dog that always hung around his grandfather’s farm when the commotion began. The dog had just let him catch up and he was about to give him a piece of jerky when he was suddenly startled as a blood curdling scream filled the air. It had come from down the street in the town square. Liam, the black smith, ran up to him and placed him in a pile of barrels and had instructed him not to move until it was safe. Liam looked at the dog and seemed to have a conversation with it after which the dog jumped into the pile and had pulled Brok to back against the bulding. Liam had the looked him in the eyes and said that the dog would keep him safe and that he should follow the dog if it beckoned him to. That was when the chaos had really begun. He remembered the man with long white hair standing on the top of one of the buildings holding a long pole. Liam the man that had always been kind to him with fire coming out of tips of his hands. He remembered the blood and how it flowed thick in the streets that night. So many dead people, so many strange creatures lying about limp on the streets. After the fighting ended the townsfolk had filtered past him and the dog. They appeared to be headed in the direction of the town hall. 

When Liam past by he looked worn and tired as if something had drained his energy. Liam had then looked directly at the dog that sat by my side and told him to take me home. I thought that was strange. Then he looked at me square in the eyes and told me to go with the dog.

Brok never saw much of Liam after that. He had served temporarily as a leader in the community but then he disappeared. Kaughman, the tall man with the long white hair, had said that Liam was a traitor and had gone into hiding to protect his life. Brok could not bring himself believe that story, he just couldn’t. Liam had always looked after brok and the other the kids. He had made toys for them in his forge.

It was shortly after that that his grandfather Kade Kastis had come home and died. That was when the dark days really began. The city was put under martial law. Brok’s father Greg had been suspicious of Kade’s death. Kade sat on the city council and voted against initiating martial law. It was only a few days later that he came home ill went to bed and never opened his eyes again.

Brok’s mind was pulled back to the present, the water that sat on the stove began to spurt and bubble as it rolled into a boil. He slowly added the meal as he stirred the water to avoid froming lumps, he hated lumpy mush so he had learned that he needed to stir constantly and slowly ad the meal while stirring. The dog that he had named Salem came in through the door and sat in begging position, Brok stepped on to the make shift step that he erected all around the house so that he could reach items that were on the shelf and took a piece of jerky down and tossed into the air. The dog jumped and grabbed it with ease and went and sat down by the stove and gnawed on his prize.

Brok ate quickly and set to work. He had a stump to clear. Much of the once community farm had been handed over under martial law to the army, thus families had to farm every piece of available land that surround their homes if they hoped to survive. Brok was more fortunate than most. His land sat on the edge of town. He had even found a small abandoned plot just over a nearby fence. It was there that he was working today. He had cleared most of the ground with exception of an old grey tree stump.

As he entered the field he reached down and picked up a pile of rich brown dirt. It felt soft and dry, and it had a slightly earthy smell to it. Not like the desert he had heard so much about but a richer smell, a smell that indicated that it yearned to for seeds to be planted in its’ depths. As Brok dropped the dirt, a gentle breeze carried it to the to the north east as it flowed away from the nearby sea.

Brok had already spent some time working on removing most of the roots of the stubborn stump. He was sure that toady was the day he would finally have the field cleared and just in time too, as the time for planting was quickly approaching. Brok spent most of the morning removing additional roots. By noon he was ready to pry the stump from the earth. With a big heave on a long branch that he had secured for this very purpose the stump slowly began to move. With the hope that his task was almost completed he put all of his weight into and the stump with a cracking and stretching sound typed over as a cloud of dirt and dust filled the air.

Brok had to brace himself from nearly falling as the stump had given way suddenly at the last moment. When stood he peered down into the hole and saw what appeared at first to be a large stone. Great he thought more lifting. But upon closer examination he realized that it was in fact a box. Curious he gingerly climbed down onto the hole so as not the push even more dirt on to the box he reached down and pulled it out of its resting position and pushed it up and into the field. After climbing out of the hole he wiped the dirt off of the box and gazed in wonder at the writing that had been carved into the lid of the box. At least that’s what he assumed the lid was. 

The box was exquisite blue granite. There were no visible seams but the box was lighter then it should be given its’ dimensions. As he lifted it up so that he could get a better look at it, he heard a slight scrape and then a thud come from inside the stone box as if something inside of it was resettling. Brok had seen the writing before he even recalled faintly seeing a similar box that his great grandmother had kept around her bedroom. As he stared at the writting he slowly began to recognize letters and the words. The box read, “This box will only open for the one true son of Gertrude von Breckstead.” The name was familiar it belonged to his great great great grandmother. She had lived during the great uprising that occurred between Cian the high regent and Darrion Bloodstone the dethroned king of Atania.

Brok ran his fingers along the outer edge of the box when he felt a small hole just small enough for his pointer finger as he gingerly put his finger into the hole he felt a little shock ripple through him travling up from the box into his finger and the through the res of his body. At that instant he also heard a subtle click and one side of the box rose just a hair up as if on a spring. Brok set the box on ground and gently open the lid to box and saw an item inside that was wrapped in a blue velvet fabric. He then folded the fabric back and beheld a large exquisite metal hammer. The hammer had writing on it and on the base of the pommel was carved a rose. 

Brok was about to reach into the box when the hair on the back of his neck stood up. For some reason his stomach also began to twist into know, and he had the most eerie feeling, not just eerie but deep, dark and full of hate. He thought that was rather strange and looked up from the box and from the corner of his eye he saw the most peculiar creature crawling towards him on all fours. He had heard of such creatures he had heard that they served the dark fey that Kaughman had always gone on and on about in the town meetings before he had disappeared, and suddenly Brok feared for his life. The creature slowly approached it fangs dripping with saliva. It was large for what appeared to be briar patch with legs. As it moved toward Brok its limbs or legs or what ever they where began twitching and moving toward Brok.

As Brok was about to give into despair a stream of fire flew past his head and hit the beast square between what seemed to be its’ eyes. Taken back the creature reeled to a fro unsure of what to do next. Just as Brok was about to turn and see what the source of the fire was a man placed a hand on his shoulder and began to mutter an incantation. Curiously, the grey dog came and nuzzled up to the man while he was uttering those strange words. 

Suddenly, Brok’s head began to spin for moment and the world ground to a halt. The only thing that moved was the man and he came around to the front of looked Brok directly into his eyes. Brok know those eyes but he could not directly place them with their owner. The man began to speak and mentioned that they only had a few moments. He said that his name was Liam and that he had been sent a message by a dear friend and he had come to find the hammer and the person that had opened it.

Brok was taken back. Was it actually Liam? Who had sent him here and how did this person know that he had this hammer, he had just opened the box a few moments ago. As his mind began to race Liam placed a hand on his shoulder and told him that they had to leave. Liam also bent down and petted the dog and said “Old man I am glad to see you are still around we have a lot to discuss.” With that, Liam picked up the box, placed one and on Brok’s shoulder and told the dog to grab on. In an instant they were in a dark very damp smelly cave.

Liam led the three of them to dry spot of the cave. Closed the box and muttered a few words and the box closed just as tight as it had been before. It was there that Liam began a long story about Brok’s great grandmother. She has served as one of Darrain’s clerics during the great uprising. She was a great healer and a great warrior. On a dark day when the battle with Cian’s forces had ravaged both sides and after spending all of her divine energy she had stayed behind in an attempt to bind the wounds of her fallen and injured comrades. She was about to begin her service when a dwarf with black hair crawled out of the injured bodies, he eyed her once and before she could react he had taken her virtue. She fought the entire time and just as he was finishing he evil deed she found a knife that was lodged into the back of an elf that lay above her head. She yanked it out and cut the man’s throat. 

She lied there for hours not wanting to move, having little desire to even live. Her body was ripe. Why now she thought? Why did this have to happen now? She had saved her life but at what cost. It was then that the man she served stood over her with tears in his eyes. He lifted the dead body off of hers. She noticed that he held his side and that blood was dripping from his hand. He reached out to her and covered the spot near her heart where the man had stabbed her. The wound healed. Darrion than reached toward her stomach and uttered a few words and then promised her that the seed of the dwarf would not take root until the spirit of that seed had become valiant and pure. With that he walked off to try and help as many as he could. He died shortly after that.

Laim looked Brok in the eyes and had said, “Brok you are the fulfillment of that prophecy. You have a great destiny ahead of you should you choose such. The hammer is a sacred key and many will kill or even give their lives to get their hands on it. You are going to have to trust me; I will need to take the key to a safe place until you are prepared to wield it as your grandmother had.” Brok was taken back, overwhelmed and speech less. He had wanted to go to this safe place. But Liam insisted that it was foolish for humans to try and live there. He said that he had a friend that could keep it for them. And that she would return when the time was right.

Liam spent the next few hours explaining what had occurred to him since he left his forge those many years ago. He mentioned the fey’s lands and the rune gates. He described the beauty of the elves. He also talked about the black dragon to the north that had helped him and his friends but that he had his own agenda in doing such. And he described the heartland forest and Emlin the elf that had saved their lives many times from the wild hunt and pumpkin head.
