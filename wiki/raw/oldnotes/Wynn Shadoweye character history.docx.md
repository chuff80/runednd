Wynn Shadoweye character history

Wynn Shadoweye was born in the Village of Fenhaven.  Like the other settlements along the foothills of the Spine, Fenhaven was constantly at war with the Lizardfolk of the Great Swamp.  Most able-bodied men in Fenhaven were called to take up the mantle of defending the community, and Belgan was no different.  He answered the call by devoting himself to the clergy of Griosagh, a minority faith that worshipped the God of Fire.  Griosagh is seen (by this minority, anyway) as a protector of the Hinfolk and the natural enemy of the waters of the Great Swamp.  Wynn Flamestaff (as he was known, back then) was a enthusiastic combatant and attached himself to several sorties that achieved great success.  He always seemed to be in the right place in the right time, and he was beginning to build quite a name for himself among his fellow villagers.
 
The Village of Fenhaven was among the first of the fronteir settlements to notice the Black Tower.  This strange structure erupted from the swamp seemingly overnight and was seen instantly as a terrible omen of things to come by the elders... some saw it as a manifestation of Meaog himself, come for revenge on all Hin.  The dread deepened when strange beings started appearing in the swamp: tall and pale, with an air of coldness about them and hollow, grim faces.  The "Pale Ones" were avoided at all costs since no one knew what they were capable of, and nobody wanted to alert these sinister creatures to the presence of nearby Fenhaven.
 
All that changed one night when Belgan Steelaxe, a respected warrior in the town, lead a patrol of four halflings a little too deep into the swamp.  The Hin were ambushed by a dozen Pale Ones.  The halfling's spears and axes were no match for the magical energies wielded by the Pale Ones, and the battle was over before it had begun.  Only Wynn survived, by feigning a hit and lying deathly still in the marsh (this isn't considered a dishonorable act among halflings of the fronteir; living to fight another day is seen as wise; foolishly sacrficing oneself to a doomed cause is not).
 
The Pale Ones were out of sight for more than an hour before Wynn drew himself up.  Night was coming and he had no idea where he was.  He struck out towards what he thought was home, and quickly learned he was very very lost.  He searched all through the night and the next day for a trail, and ate all his rations.  Still, nothing.  By nightfall of the second day hunger gnawed at him and he felt his situation getting worse.
 
On the third day he stumbled through the swamp, and found nothing.  It wasn't until dark began to fall that he came across a body floating in the swamp... he looked closer, and it was a fallen Pale One!  The creature had giant clawmarks across it's chest and back and lay face-down in the water.
 
One must understand that a Halfling of the Spine views a recently-dead, floating humanoid carcass in a vastly different way than members of other races.  Wynn was succumbing to starvation, and here was food.  He was lost, and here was a creature who had knowledge of the deep swamp.  All Halflings have the ability to access some of the memories of sentient creatures they consume; perhaps eating this being would give Wynn knowledge of how to get home.  At the very least, it would allow him to survive a few more days until he found his own way out.  Without giving it too much more thought, he began in.
 
**************************************************************
 
One day later Wynn returned home.  He had been gone almost a week, and he relayed the news of the slaughter.  He described how he got lost, and came across the body.  Some in the community were shocked to hear what came next; it is considered taboo to eat a non-halfling, even if it did happen from time to time.  Others accepted it the way one might accept cutting of one's arm to escape a trap, or killing a stranger in order to save one's child.  It was regrettable - even offensive - but you might do the same in the same situation, and at least you made it out alive.
 
That all began to change on Wynn's second day in the village.  He had been unable to sleep the night before; whenever he closed his eyes visions of a terrible place assaulted him and he was jarred awake.  He began to hear a whispering murmur in his head that told him secrets: what other people were thinking, answers to questions he had never heard before, and rantings in a dark, twisted language he slowly grew to understand.  Food tasted bland and chalky, no matter what he ate; and no matter what he ate it was only a matter of time before it came back up.  Yet he wasn't hungry or thirsty; his hunger appeared to be satisfied by the thoughts - the dark thoughts, the fears - of those around him.  Even when they weren't consciously thinking of these things they fueled him, like a low heat.  When he was near someone in the grips of terror, he felt sated and content.
 
Wynn began to suspect he had adopted more than just a few of the Pale One's memories - he sensed that the creature's very essence was within him, trying to get out, and slowly taking over of his body.  The final straw came two days later - now a week without real sleep, food or water - when he grew uncharacteristically angry at a fellow tribesman and inadvertently launched a bolt of dark magic at him.  The halfling survived to tell the council that he saw Wynn's eyes go black as he cast this magic, and declare a new name for Wynn Flamestaff, Priest of Griosagh: Wynn Shadoweye, Walks-with-darkness.
 
At that council meeting it was decided that Wynn should leave Fenhaven.  The elders gave no one reason, although Wynn knows it was to protect both himself and his community.  Wynn accepted his exile with bittersweet feelings... he knew it was best for his town and that he needed to leave in order to find a way to rid himself of this creature, but he would miss his home and his family and feared for their defense against the Lizardfolk and Pale Ones with one fewer patrol to guard the borders.
 
Nearly as soon as Wynn left Fenhaven out of sight the voices increased fearfully in intensity, pushing him onward down the road and screaming for release.  Over the weeks travelling on the road it is safe to say that Wynn is losing his grip on sanity [I like to think he was a high-wisdom cleric when this story started and has slowly been suffering from 3rd-edition style wisdom drain.  ; )]  The voices increasingly distract him from conversations, observations, and his ability to focus.  He has been walking to Atania for weeks, battling the monster within himself and trying to keep his secret safe with the merchants and travellers he meets.  He keeps moving, being sure not to linger long enough with any one group that they may learn of his powers and his past.
 
He arrived in Atania two nights ago and has been overcome with the plight of the people there.  He knows of the resistance and the empire although he has not made formal contact with the resistance (he's putting himself out there to be found, but also trying to keep a low-profile as a new guy in town).  Atania is the biggest city Wynn has ever visited and he is a little overwhelmed... even as the darkness inside him feasts on the palpable tension in the air.
